# Post-harvest losses and the case for better storage decisions

## Why we looked into this

A crop can lose value after it has been harvested. Handling, sorting, packaging, transport and storage conditions can all affect quantity and quality. This matters when a farmer is deciding whether to sell immediately or hold produce for a later sale.

## A published reference

The Ministry of Food Processing Industries commissioned a study titled *Study to Determine Post-Harvest Losses of Agri Produces in India*, conducted by NABARD Consultancy Services (NABCONS). Government summaries of the study report estimated loss ranges of 6.02–15.05% for fruits and 4.87–11.61% for vegetables.

These are study estimates across the commodities and areas covered. They should not be treated as the expected loss for every crop, farm, district or storage method.

## How this connects to Kisan Setu

The project aims to help farmers compare selling now with storing some or all of their produce. A useful comparison should consider:

- the crop and its condition at harvest;
- how long it may be stored safely;
- the type and cost of storage available;
- expected handling, transport and loading costs;
- possible quality or quantity loss;
- the farmer's need for cash and the uncertainty of future prices.

The platform should avoid giving a simple instruction to wait for a higher price. A higher future price may not cover storage costs or the risk of deterioration. For perishable crops, the time available may be especially limited.

## How we may use the findings

The study can help explain why post-harvest handling and storage deserve attention in the project. It should not be used as a fixed loss percentage in the decision calculator. Any crop-specific estimate used by the prototype needs a suitable source, local validation and a clear explanation of its limits.
